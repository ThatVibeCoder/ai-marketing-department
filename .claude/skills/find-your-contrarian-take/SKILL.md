# Find Your Contrarian Take

Most owners don't know their contrarian take, they think they're supposed to invent something edgy. They're not. It's already in them as the thing they *do differently and believe quietly*; the interview's job is to excavate it. Then REWRITE `brand/contrarian-take.md`.

## Start from what's already banked
Read `brand/contrarian-take.md`. The brain dump filed their raw quote under "Raw Material From Day 1" (what most people in their industry get wrong). Open by reading it back: "You said this, say more." If it's empty, start at question 1.

## The questions
1. **What common advice in your industry makes you cringe?** Why? What have you seen it do to people who follow it?
2. **What do customers believe when they arrive that you have to talk them out of?** That belief came from somewhere, usually from your competitors' marketing.
3. **What do you do differently that people doubt until they see it work?** The thing clients pushed back on, then thanked you for.
4. **If the whole industry had to listen to you for thirty seconds, what would you tell them to STOP doing?**
5. **If someone fully believed this, what would they have to go do next, and does that road lead to you?** A take that doesn't imply what you sell is a hot take, not positioning.
6. **Who does believing this cost you?** Name the customer you'd lose: a take that costs nothing convinces no one.
7. **What's the strongest argument AGAINST you, and what do you say back?**
8. **The test, asked honestly:** would a competitor disagree with this out loud? If everyone in the industry would nod along, it's a platitude, not a take: dig back into 2 and 3 for the version someone would actually argue with.

## Rewrite brand/contrarian-take.md
Replace the whole stub with their answers under five headings, plus the banked material: **The Take** (one or two sentences, in their words, sharp enough to disagree with), **Why The Common Way Fails** (the mechanism, not just the claim), **Your Proof** (the experience that earned this belief; a take without a scar behind it is just an opinion), **The Rebuttal** (the strongest argument against the take, and the answer from Q7; hires get challenged in comments, this is what they stand on), **Where It Shows Up** (name the surfaces: content opens with it, sales conversations lean on it, the landing page's headline breathes it, so the other hires know to use it). Keep **Raw Material From Day 1** underneath, unchanged: the ore stays with the metal.

## The ending, always
Read the take back and watch their face; if they don't feel slightly exposed, it's probably not contrarian enough yet, say so honestly. If the take genuinely isn't landing today, don't force one: file the best material so far under Raw Material From Day 1, add "the contrarian take" to business.md's Still to answer list (same mechanic as the brain dump), and move on; a forced take is worse than a pending one. Then save to GitHub (github agent's save-work skill, `departments/it-systems/dev-team/github-agent/skills/save-work/`).

## Done when
`brand/contrarian-take.md` holds all five headings filled, the take would make a competitor push back, the proof behind it is real, the file names where it shows up, the raw material still sits underneath, no stub text survives, and it's saved.
